How UK statutory late payment interest works

Who can claim it

You can claim if you are a business (including a sole trader or freelancer) and you supplied goods or services on credit to another business or a public sector body, and they paid late. It does not cover sales to private individuals.

When a payment counts as late

  • If you agreed a payment date, the payment is late from the day after that date.
  • If you did not agree one, the law sets a default: 30 days after the customer received the invoice or the goods or services, whichever is later.
  • If your contract already sets its own fair late-payment terms, those usually apply instead of the Act.

The interest rate

Statutory interest is 8% a year plus the Bank of England base rate. The base rate used is fixed in six-month blocks: for debts that became overdue between 1 January and 30 June, use the base rate on the previous 31 December; for debts overdue between 1 July and 31 December, use the rate on 30 June. That is why this calculator asks you to enter the base rate rather than assuming it.

The calculation

It is simple interest, not compound:

  1. Yearly interest = amount owed × (8% + base rate)
  2. Daily interest = yearly interest ÷ 365
  3. Interest owed = daily interest × number of days late

Example: £5,000 owed, base rate 4%, 30 days late. Rate = 12%. Yearly interest = £600. Daily = £1.64. Interest = £49.32. Add £70 fixed compensation for a total claim of £119.32 on top of the invoice.

Fixed compensation

  • Debt under £1,000: £40
  • Debt of £1,000 to £9,999.99: £70
  • Debt of £10,000 or more: £100

You can claim this once per late invoice. You may also be able to claim reasonable extra costs of recovering the debt if they are higher than the fixed sum.

How to use it in practice

Tell your client in writing that the invoice is late and that statutory interest and compensation can now be added. Keep the tone calm and factual. Mentioning the Act often gets an invoice paid, and you can choose to waive the extra if they pay promptly.

FAQ

Questions

Can I charge late payment interest to a consumer?

No. The Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 only covers business-to-business debts, including sales to the public sector. It does not apply to sales to private individuals.

What interest rate applies to late commercial payments in the UK?

Statutory interest is 8% a year plus the Bank of England base rate. The base rate used is fixed for six months at a time, so check the rate that applied on the relevant reference date on the Bank of England website.

How much fixed compensation can I claim?

On top of interest you can claim a fixed sum per late invoice: £40 for debts under £1,000, £70 for debts of £1,000 to £9,999.99, and £100 for debts of £10,000 or more.

Do I have to charge statutory interest?

No. It is your right, not an obligation. Many freelancers mention it in a reminder and waive it if the client pays promptly.
