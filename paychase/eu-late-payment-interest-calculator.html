How EU late payment interest works

Who it covers

The Late Payment Directive protects businesses, including freelancers and sole traders, that sell goods or services to other businesses or to public authorities in the EU. It does not cover sales to consumers.

Payment terms

  • Public authorities should generally pay within 30 days (60 days in limited cases).
  • Businesses should pay within the agreed term. Terms longer than 60 days are only allowed if expressly agreed and not grossly unfair to the supplier.
  • If no date was agreed, interest generally starts 30 days after the customer receives the invoice or the goods or services.

The interest rate

Statutory interest is the reference rate plus at least 8 percentage points. In euro countries, the reference rate is the ECB main refinancing operations rate on the first day of the half-year (1 January or 1 July), and it stays fixed for those six months. Enter the rate that applies to your debt; this calculator does not assume a value.

The calculation

This calculator uses simple daily interest: amount owed × annual rate ÷ 365 × days late. Some countries use a different day count, so treat the result as an estimate.

Example: €10,000 owed, reference rate 2.15%, margin 8 points, 100 days late. Rate = 10.15%. Interest = €278.08. Add €40 compensation for €318.08 on top of the invoice.

Fixed compensation

You are entitled to at least €40 per late invoice as compensation for recovery costs, without needing to prove them. Reasonable recovery costs above €40 may also be claimable.

FAQ

Questions

What is the late payment interest rate in the EU?

Under Directive 2011/7/EU, statutory interest is the reference rate (for euro countries, the European Central Bank main refinancing rate) plus at least 8 percentage points. Each country sets the exact rule in its national law, and some go higher.

How much compensation can I claim for a late invoice in the EU?

At least €40 per late invoice as fixed compensation for recovery costs, without having to prove the costs. You may also claim reasonable recovery costs above that.

Does the EU Late Payment Directive apply to consumers?

No. It covers commercial transactions between businesses, and between businesses and public authorities.

Which reference rate should I enter?

For euro area countries, the ECB main refinancing operations rate in force on the first calendar day of the relevant half-year (1 January or 1 July). Countries outside the euro use their own central bank rate. Check the current figure before relying on the result.
