Final payment reminder letter template

A firm but respectful final reminder for an unpaid invoice, for when friendly reminders haven't worked. Send it as an email, a PDF attachment or a printed letter.

This is a general template, not legal advice. If you are planning legal action, check your local rules or speak to an adviser first.

Final reminder letter
[Your name / business name] [Your address] [Your email] | [Your phone] [Date] [Client name] [Client company] [Client address] Subject: Final reminder - invoice [Invoice #] for [Amount], overdue since [Due date] Dear [Client name], I am writing about invoice [Invoice #] dated [Invoice date] for [Amount], for [short description of the work]. Payment was due on [Due date], and the invoice is now [X] days overdue. I sent reminders on [Date 1], [Date 2] and [Date 3], but I have not yet received payment or an explanation of the delay. A copy of the invoice is attached. Please pay the outstanding balance of [Amount] by [Date, for example 14 days from today] using the details below: [Bank / payment details] If there is a problem with the invoice or the work, or if you need to pay in instalments, please contact me before that date so we can agree a way forward. I would much prefer to resolve this directly with you. If I do not hear from you, I will need to consider the other options available to me to recover the amount owed. Thank you for your attention to this. I value our working relationship and hope we can settle this quickly. Yours sincerely, [Your name] [Business name]

Before you send

  • Replace every [square bracket].
  • Attach a copy of the invoice.
  • Give a clear date to pay by, usually 7 to 14 days.

Optional line for UK business clients: "As this is a business-to-business debt, statutory interest and fixed compensation under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 may also be added." Work out the figures with the UK calculator (or the EU calculator for EU clients).

What to do if the client still doesn't pay

  1. Re-read your contract or proposal. Check the agreed payment terms, any late fee or interest clause, and what it says about pausing work, ownership of the work and disputes.
  2. Make sure there's no genuine dispute. Ask directly whether anything about the work or invoice is holding up payment. Fixing a small issue is faster than any legal route.
  3. Offer a payment plan. Two or three instalments that actually get paid beat a full amount that never arrives. Confirm any plan in writing.
  4. Try a phone or video call. A short, calm conversation often unblocks what messages haven't.
  5. Use the platform if you were hired on one. If the job came through Upwork, Fiverr or a similar marketplace, use their payment protection and dispute process, and keep payments on the platform.
  6. Look at small claims options. Many countries have a simpler, cheaper court process for smaller debts. In England and Wales you can make a court claim for money online. For cross-border claims within the EU, there is the European Small Claims Procedure. Elsewhere, including Pakistan, India and the Gulf, speak to a local lawyer or adviser about the right route.
  7. Keep good records. Save the contract, invoice, proof of delivery, every reminder (with dates) and any replies. Screenshots of WhatsApp chats help too.
  8. Protect yourself next time. Ask for a deposit or milestone payments, set clear payment terms up front, and send reminders before the due date, not only after.

Stay professional throughout. Don't threaten, harass, publicly shame a client or contact their family or employer: it can backfire and may be unlawful.

FAQ

Questions

What should a final payment reminder letter include?

The invoice number, date and amount, the original due date, how many days it is overdue, the dates of earlier reminders, a clear date to pay by, how to pay, and an invitation to get in touch if there is a problem or a payment plan is needed.

How long should I give a client to pay after a final reminder?

Seven to fourteen days is common and reasonable. Some court pre-action rules expect a set period before you start a claim, so check the rules where you are.

Can I add late payment interest to a final reminder?

If you invoiced a business in the UK or the EU, you may be entitled to statutory interest and fixed compensation. Use the UK or EU calculator to estimate it and mention it factually in your letter.
